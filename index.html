Why BlueDot

01
We both put humans first
Most of AI safety optimizes the research. BlueDot starts from people. So do I.
›

A lot of AI safety work is about advancing the research itself. BlueDot starts somewhere else: with the humans on the receiving end. That is my starting point too. I am an economics major, so I have always measured work by what it does to people, and you can see it in how I decide. On a project helping a pharma giant automate roles, the moment that mattered most to me was seeing how many people it would displace, and changing the plan. Human-first is not a line for me. It is the value I make decisions against, and it is the one BlueDot leads with.

02
AI safety matters, to both of us
A system that improves itself is a different kind of risk, and it scales with people.
›

AI safety matters to me because we are building something that can improve itself, and the danger grows with how deeply it reaches into people's lives. That makes it one of the defining problems of this decade, and it is the exact problem BlueDot exists to work on. This is where BlueDot and I converge: a risk big enough that getting it right is a global outcome, not a local one.

03
We both care who is in the room
Representation over groupthink. BlueDot field-builds for it. I have lived it.
›

BlueDot answers the AGI problem by widening the field, not narrowing it. It upskills the people least equipped to face AI, most of the world, and deliberately pulls in expertise from outside the usual circles. You can see it in who they target and the alumni they reach. That matters to me because I have lived it. Studying and working across very different countries taught me what groupthink costs and what representation is worth. I am not the standard candidate either. Outside my master's I do not come from a technical background, and I think that is exactly the point.

And here is what I come to get. BlueDot began as a few people in a reading room in Cambridge, and that detail tells me how much its founders care. Passion like that is either there or it is not, and it is the best catalyst I know for real work. They have since scaled that room into something global, which makes them exactly the people I want to be led by while I learn how to lead myself.

The logic is simple, and it is the effective-altruism logic I believe in: put your effort where it returns the most. On my own I can interview maybe five thousand people in a lifetime and write up what I learn. Joining an organization that multiplies that effort beats scattering it, and I have seen what scattered effort costs. Pakistan is the most charitable country in the world and still has some of the most people in poverty, because everyone solves the same problem alone. BlueDot gives me the funding, the people, and the guidance to learn AI through a human lens from every angle, and then go deep where it matters. That platform is the time and the reach I am here for.
