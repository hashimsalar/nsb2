A lot of AI safety work is about advancing the research itself. BlueDot starts somewhere else, with the humans on the receiving end. That is my starting point too. I am an economics major, so I have always measured work by what it does to people, and you can see it in how I decide. On a project helping a pharma giant automate roles, the moment that mattered most to me was seeing how many people it would displace, and changing the plan. Human-first is the value I make decisions against, and it is the one BlueDot leads with.

AI safety matters to me because we are building something that can improve itself, and the danger grows with how deeply it reaches into people's lives. That makes it one of the defining problems of this decade, and it is the exact problem BlueDot exists to work on. This is where BlueDot and I converge, a risk big enough that getting it right is a global outcome, not a local one.

BlueDot answers the AGI problem by widening the field, not narrowing it. It upskills the people least equipped to face AI, most of the world, and deliberately pulls in expertise from outside the usual circles. That matters to me because I have lived it. Studying and working across very different countries taught me what groupthink costs and what representation is worth. I am not the standard candidate either, and I think that is exactly the point.

ProblemGLP-1 users were experiencing side effects, but the support around them was still catching up. Much of what existed was generic wellness advice rather than nutrition support built specifically for people using these medications.
DiagnosisGLP-1 medications were moving faster than the support around them. People were dealing with side effects in daily life, while most nutrition support still came from general wellness products and broad advice. Helpful support had to be specific enough to be measurably useful to patients and scientifically sound for doctors to stand behind it.
SolutionI led a first-to-market, science-backed nutrition support product from 0 to 1, working across research, physician input, product positioning, launch, and clinical-facing outreach.
Outcome4,000+ GLP-1 users in the U.S. were reached, with 77% of surveyed users reporting short-term improvement in side-effect management. The product was also presented to clinical and wellness audiences through AmSpa and featured at the National Obesity Medicine Conference.
ProblemFlood-risk residents in the Bay Area were being sent important safety information by mail, but the material was not reliably reaching attention. Many residents did not remember receiving it or said it may have been thrown out with other mail.
DiagnosisThe weak point was the delivery channel. Calling residents showed that a mailed flyer could technically be delivered and still fail to inform. People needed more chances to encounter the same information in places they frequented and trusted.
SolutionI redesigned the distribution strategy beyond mailers, adding higher-trust community touchpoints such as schools, religious spaces, and local shops.
Outcome150,000+ flood-risk residents were reached through additional community-based distribution channels, making critical safety information more likely to be seen before an emergency.
ProblemGovernment community kitchens in Islamabad were serving underprivileged people on a large scale, but most kitchens were operating in deeply unhygienic conditions.
DiagnosisThe issue arose during a field visit with the Food Department. That morning, a private bakery had been fined for failing health standards. Later the same day, a government kitchen serving people with far fewer choices was in worse condition. The same hygiene expectations applied to private businesses also needed to be visible in public kitchens, especially where people had few alternatives.
SolutionI raised this with the commissioner and argued that public kitchens should exemplify hygienic food service, especially because the people depending on them had the least ability to choose alternatives. The issue was taken seriously, and stronger kitchen safety standards were introduced as enforceable SOPs, with daily checks.
Outcome90,000+ underprivileged people in Islamabad had access to more hygienic food each day through community kitchens operating under stronger safety standards.
ProblemMinerva's applicant data showed limited representation from Karachi, despite the city being one of Pakistan's largest education markets, home to many capable students.
DiagnosisThe constraint was access to information. Students could not consider a competitive global education pathway if it had not been presented to them clearly, directly, and in a locally relevant way.
SolutionI led a self-initiated outreach sprint across 32 schools in Karachi, speaking with students and counselors about Minerva as a competitive, low-cost U.S. university option.
Outcome~1,200 Karachi students were informed of a clearer pathway to global education, contributing to 400% application growth from the city.
ProblemPublic health data showed that many youth deaths could be prevented if basic first-aid education was given early. In many public-school settings, students were not being taught these skills.
DiagnosisThe gap was partly curricular and partly linguistic. First aid was not consistently part of what students learned in school, and usable Urdu learning material was difficult to find. A student could search for help and still be left with content that was too technical, too scattered, or in the wrong language.
SolutionI built an Urdu first-aid resource with input from doctors and turned it into a five-session learning program for students across public schools.
Outcome67 students across 25 schools learned first-aid basics through material designed to be easier to understand, remember, and share. The resource was also made publicly accessible.
ProblemExpatrio wanted to move toward B Corp certification and strengthen its sustainability practices, but the work required more than a general commitment to being greener.
DiagnosisThe company needed to see where its practices already met B Corp standards, where evidence was missing, and which changes would make the largest difference across governance, workers, community, environment, and customers.
SolutionI worked with two teammates to audit Expatrio's practices against the B Corp framework, identify gaps, and recommend changes that could improve both the company's score and its day-to-day sustainability performance.
OutcomeThe assessment gave Expatrio a clearer path toward B Corp readiness and greener operations, with recommendations that supported an estimated ~60% improvement from its baseline assessment score.
ProblemStudents, including myself, trying to understand Pakistan's political and economic future often had to rely on public commentary, second-hand summaries, or material that did not let them ask their own questions.
DiagnosisThe useful knowledge was often held by people who had made decisions, studied them closely, or advised from inside institutions. Students needed a way to reach those people directly, ask harder questions, and preserve the conversation for others to use.
SolutionI recruited and interviewed 40+ high-caliber leaders through cold outreach and relationship-building, including Nobel laureates and senior policymakers. I designed and moderated live student Q&A conversations, with questions built to draw out answers beyond prepared talking points.
OutcomeThe series gave young Pakistanis direct access to senior public voices before live student audiences. Archived episodes averaged 3,835 views and became a research resource beyond the original event.
