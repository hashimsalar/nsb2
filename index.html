ProblemGLP-1 users were dealing with side effects in daily life, but the nutrition support around them was generic wellness advice rather than supplementation built for people on these medications.
DiagnosisThe industry was moving faster than the science-backed products serving it. Useful nutritional support had to be specific enough to be measurably felt by patients and rigorous enough for physicians to stand behind.
SolutionI led a first-to-market, science-backed nutrition support product from 0 to 1, working across research, physician input, positioning, launch, and clinical-facing outreach.
Outcome4,000+ US GLP-1 users reached, with 77% of surveyed users reporting improvement in side-effect management. Presented to clinical and wellness audiences through AmSpa and featured at the National Obesity Medicine Conference.
ProblemStudents trying to research or understand Pakistan's political and economic future had to rely on public commentary and second-hand summaries, with limited ways to ask their own questions.
DiagnosisThe useful knowledge sat with people who made or advised on decisions from inside institutions. Students needed primary access and a record they could return to for research.
SolutionI recruited 40+ leaders through cold outreach, including Nobel laureates and senior policymakers, and moderated live student Q&As with questions built to draw out answers beyond talking points. Watch on YouTube →
Outcome~80 live participants per session and 3,835 average views per archived episode, giving young Pakistanis direct access to leaders they wouldn't otherwise reach.
ProblemFlood-risk residents in the Bay Area were being sent safety information by mail, but it wasn't reaching them. Many didn't remember receiving it or said it was thrown out with junk mail.
DiagnosisThe weak point was the delivery channel. A mailed flyer could arrive and still fail to inform. People needed more chances to encounter the information in places they trusted.
SolutionI added distribution points at places of worship, schools, and grocery stores, so each household had multiple chances to see the information.
Outcome150,000+ flood-risk residents reached through the expanded distribution, making safety information more likely to be seen before an emergency.
ProblemGovernment community kitchens in Islamabad were feeding underprivileged people at scale, but most were operating in deeply unhygienic conditions.
DiagnosisPublic kitchens weren't being held to the same hygiene bar as private businesses, even though their users had the least ability to go elsewhere. On a field visit, a private bakery was fined for failing health standards the same morning we saw a government kitchen in worse condition.
SolutionI raised this with the government, arguing that public kitchens should exemplify hygienic food service. Stronger safety standards were adopted as enforceable SOPs with daily checks.
Outcome90,000+ underprivileged people in Islamabad gained access to more hygienic food each day through kitchens operating under the new standards.
ProblemPublic health data showed many youth deaths could be prevented if basic first aid were taught early, but many public schools weren't teaching it.
DiagnosisThe gap was partly curricular and partly linguistic. First aid wasn't part of the syllabus, and usable Urdu material was hard to find. A student searching for help was left with content that was too technical, too scattered, or in the wrong language.
SolutionI built an Urdu first-aid resource with input from doctors and piloted it as a five-session program across public schools.
Outcome67 students across 25 schools learned first-aid basics through the pilot, with the resource made publicly accessible for continued use.
ProblemMinerva's applicant data showed limited representation from Karachi, despite the city being one of Pakistan's largest education markets and home to many capable students.
DiagnosisThe constraint was access to information. Students couldn't consider a competitive global pathway if it hadn't been presented to them clearly, directly, and in a locally relevant way.
SolutionI led a self-initiated sprint across 32 Karachi schools, speaking with students and counselors about Minerva as a competitive, low-cost US option.
Outcome1,200+ Karachi students were introduced to a clearer pathway to global education, contributing to 400% application growth from the city.
ProblemExpatrio wanted to move toward B Corp certification and strengthen its sustainability practices, but the work required more than a general commitment.
DiagnosisB Lab's platform could tell Expatrio their current score but not which changes would increase it most, because the per-question weights weren't visible outside the platform.
SolutionI worked with two teammates to reverse-engineer the scoring model from B Lab's published methodology, rebuild it in Excel, and simulate how specific changes would move Expatrio's score.
OutcomeRoughly 60% projected improvement to Expatrio's baseline B Corp score, delivered as a prioritized set of recommendations the company could act on.

05Econometrics
Hosting the Olympics: The Secret to a Country's Economic Prosperity?
Applied synthetic-control methodology to estimate the causal effect of hosting the 2000 Sydney Olympics on Australia's GDP per capita. Found a brief uptick in the hosting year followed by a sustained decline, with robustness checks ruling out anticipation and spillover, adding weight to the case against the economics of mega-events.

Built the economic case for Pakistan joining the WTO Information Technology Agreement, drawing on trade theory, labor-market analysis, and the Solow-Swan model. Challenged protectionist arguments by quantifying the cost of technological exclusion and showing GDP gains would outpace lost import-tax revenue by orders of magnitude.

Built a profit-maximization model for Southwest Airlines using Excel Solver, incorporating dynamic pricing, demand forecasting, and fleet assignment across aircraft types.
